Application Fit

What are stand-up pouches used for?

The same pouch shape can require very different films, seals and features depending on the packed product.

Coffee & Tea

Confirm roast condition, aroma barrier and gas release before specifying a one-way valve, zipper and metallized or foil-based structure.

Snacks & Dried Foods

Match the moisture, oxygen and light barrier to product sensitivity, oil content, shelf life and distribution environment.

Pet Food & Treats

Confirm packed weight, fat or aroma content, puncture risk, zipper use and the regulations for the destination market.

Powders & Supplements

Review powder contamination at the zipper and seal area, fill method, moisture sensitivity, opening size and consumer use.

Feature Selection

Choose the Closure and Pouch Features

Features must work with the film structure, pouch dimensions, filling equipment and consumer-opening sequence.

Press-to-Close Zipper

A common resealable option for multi-use packs. Zipper position, opening clearance and top-seal area are confirmed on the dieline.

Powder-Resistant Zipper

Evaluate zipper geometry when fine powder can interfere with closure. The finished pouch still requires filling and reclosure checks.

One-Way Degassing Valve

Used for products such as freshly roasted coffee when gas must exit the package. Valve type and position depend on the product and packing process.

Window, Tear Notch & Hang Hole

Balance product visibility, opening convenience and retail display with the required barrier and available print area.

Barrier Engineering

How to Select the Film Structure

Clear Structures

Use when product visibility is important and the validated oxygen, moisture and light requirements allow a transparent construction.

Metallized Structures

A candidate when stronger moisture and light protection is required without the complete opacity and construction of an aluminum-foil laminate.

Foil-Laminate Structures

Evaluate for demanding oxygen, moisture or light targets. Confirm flex-crack risk, sealing performance, filling process and distribution conditions.

PE-Based Structures

A design-for-recycling path may use compatible PE layers, but performance, collection access and local labeling rules must be confirmed.

Buyer Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a stand-up pouch?

A stand-up pouch is a flexible package with an expandable bottom gusset that supports the pouch when filled. The bottom construction, dimensions and film structure are selected for the product and filling method.

What is the MOQ for custom stand-up pouches?

The standard MOQ is 500 PCS per custom size and artwork. Final quantity requirements depend on the film, printing method, zipper, valve and other features.

How is the barrier film selected?

Film selection starts with product composition, sensitivity to oxygen, moisture and light, target shelf life, fill temperature, sealing process and distribution conditions. Candidate structures must be confirmed with supplier data and product compatibility or shelf-life testing.

Can stand-up pouches include a zipper, window or coffee valve?

Yes, subject to the selected structure. Options can include press-to-close or powder-resistant zippers, clear windows, tear notches, hang holes and one-way degassing valves. Valve selection should match the product's gas-release behavior and packing process.

How is food-contact compliance confirmed for stand-up pouches?

Food-contact compliance should not be treated as one blanket approval for every pouch. The exact film layers, adhesives, inks and additives must be suitable for the intended food type, contact time, temperature and market. Buyers should request compliance declarations or supporting documentation for the final construction.

How are pouch size, samples and production schedules confirmed?

Provide the product, net weight, bulk density where relevant, fill method, desired width, height and bottom gusset, features, artwork and destination. Sample cost, sample time and the production schedule are quoted after these details are reviewed.
