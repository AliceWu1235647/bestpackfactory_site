Custom Chocolate and Confectionery Gift Boxes: Buyer Sourcing Guide

Published August 20, 2026 · Buyer guide · By BestPackFactory

Quick answer

For chocolate and confectionery, specify food-contact-safe boards and inks, a compartmented tray (greyboard or food-grade PET) that separates pieces, and an interior barrier or liner where oil migration is a risk. Rigid gift boxes quote from 500-1,000 pieces; folding cartons with trays from 3,000. Samples in 7-10 days, bulk 20-30 days. For holiday peaks, approve pre-production samples 8-10 weeks before ship date.

Food-contact basics to confirm

The box itself rarely touches the chocolate when a tray or wrapper separates them, but buyers should still request supplier declarations on board, inks and coatings. Ask for compliance documentation for the target market and consult a specialist: one approval does not cover every country. Avoid recycled fibers in direct contact with unwrapped product, and confirm whether a food-grade liner is required for your filling process.

Compartment trays and piece protection

A die-cut greyboard tray with individual compartments (16-24 cells for pralines) keeps pieces from rubbing, and a fluted or honeycomb layer adds crush resistance. For chocolate with fillings, a thin food-grade PET or PP tray under the greyboard prevents oil wicking. Cell size should be piece footprint plus 1-2 mm; test that pieces drop in and lift out cleanly at production line speed.

Shelf life, temperature and transit

Heat is the enemy: ask the factory how the box behaves in a 40-50°C container, and confirm the corrugated outer carton offers 4-8 kg/cm² edge crush for stacking. For seasonal launches, avoid finishes that soften under warmth and specify matte or soft-touch lamination over the print. Include a packed heat and drop test in the approval protocol, not just a visual sample.

Planning for holiday peaks

Chocolate demand spikes at Lunar New Year, Valentine's Day and Christmas. Lock artwork 6-8 weeks ahead, place bulk orders 10-12 weeks before the event, and order 3-5% extra to cover QC rejects and in-transit damage. Ask the supplier to hold your dielines and tooling so reorders skip the setup phase.

Buyer checklist

  1. Request supplier declarations on food-contact board, inks and coatings for target markets
  2. Specify compartment tray cell size (piece plus 1-2 mm) and test fill at line speed
  3. Add a food-grade liner or barrier where oil migration is a risk
  4. Run packed heat (40-50°C) and drop tests before bulk approval
  5. Order 3-5% overage and place bulk orders 10-12 weeks before holiday peaks

Frequently asked questions

Does chocolate packaging need food-grade certification?

If the box separates the product with a tray or wrapper, contact is limited, but buyers should still request supplier declarations and consult a compliance advisor for each market. One certificate rarely covers all countries.

What tray prevents pralines from moving?

A die-cut greyboard tray with individual cells sized at piece footprint plus 1-2 mm, plus a fluted or honeycomb layer for crush resistance. Use a thin food-grade PET or PP tray under it when fillings can wick oil.

When should chocolate orders be placed for Christmas?

Place bulk orders 10-12 weeks before the event and approve pre-production samples 8-10 weeks ahead. Add 3-5% overage for QC rejects and transit damage.

What the Spec Means for Buyers

Chocolate gift boxes combine food-contact safety with premium presentation. Rigid boxes from 1200/1500/1800 gsm greyboard wrapped in 157 gsm C1S art paper or 120 gsm specialty paper carry the outer presentation; food-safe paperboard trays or molded pulp inserts hold the chocolates. Finishes of matte lamination, soft-touch, hot foil and embossing set the gift tone. The box passes a 24 h static load test.
