Custom Burger Boxes and Fast-Food Packaging: What Buyers Should Specify

Published August 22, 2026 · Buyer guide · By BestPackFactory

Quick answer

For burger and fast-food packaging, confirm grease resistance at serving temperature, closure strength, stacking behavior and food-safe inks. Specify board, coating, venting and print, then test with the real hot product in the expected delivery condition before mass production.

Keep heat and grease in the spec

Hot burgers release steam and oil. The box must keep the bun from becoming soggy while holding heat. Venting, coating and board stiffness all matter; test the complete hot meal, not an empty box.

Closures that survive delivery

A box that pops open in a delivery bag creates cold food and complaints. Confirm the closure holds under stack pressure and vibration. Compare tuck, lock-bottom and sealed formats for assembly speed and reliability.

Printing that stays clean

Full-color brand print on a greasy, warm box can smear or weaken if inks and coatings are not matched. Confirm ink suitability and rub resistance. Keep barcode and allergen-label areas clear of coating interference.

Request the right documents

Ask for material declaration, coating details and food-contact documentation applicable to your market. Keep approved samples and specification records for repeat orders.

Buyer checklist

  1. Specify serving temperature and time
  2. Test closure under stack pressure
  3. Confirm grease and rub resistance
  4. Verify food-safe inks and coatings
  5. Keep approved samples for reorders

Frequently asked questions

Can burger boxes be made from recycled board?

Yes, if the grade, inks and coatings meet your food-contact requirements; appearance can vary between lots.

Do I need venting in burger boxes?

Venting reduces steam and soggy buns, but balance it against heat retention for your delivery time.

What documents should I request?

Request material and coating declarations plus any food-contact documentation required for your destination market.

What the Spec Means for Buyers

Burger boxes are specified on board weight, food safety and print. Kraft or white cardboard at 250–400 gsm with food-safe coatings handles the standard burger box; higher weights or E-flute are used for stacked or heavier items. Printing is offset, flexo or digital at 1–8 colors; finishes include matte or gloss lamination and soft-touch. MOQ is 500 PCS per size and artwork, sampling 5–7 working days after artwork confirmation. FDA-compliant ink and material statements are available on request.
