Custom Packaging Import Duties and Tariffs: What Buyers Should Check

Published August 27, 2026 · Buyer guide · By BestPackFactory

Quick answer

Import duties depend on the HS code classification of your packaging, the country of origin and the importing market. Confirm the correct HS code, origin and valuation with your customs broker before ordering, and include duties in your landed-cost calculation. Classification errors create delays and penalties.

Classification comes first

Paper boxes, rigid boxes and flexible pouches fall under different HS codes with different duty rates. Confirm the classification for your product with a customs broker before quoting landed costs.

Origin changes the rate

Trade agreements can reduce or remove duties based on origin and rule-of-origin criteria. Confirm the documentation needed to claim preferential rates.

Value what you declare

Customs values goods on the declared transaction value. Ensure invoices and declared values are consistent and complete to avoid delays.

Plan duties into pricing

Duties, VAT/GST and fees are part of landed cost. Model them before pricing to market, and ask the supplier for the documents (invoice, packing list, COO) your broker needs.

Buyer checklist

  1. Confirm HS code with broker
  2. Check origin and trade agreements
  3. Align declared values
  4. Model duties in landed cost
  5. Request export documents

Frequently asked questions

Who determines the HS code?

The importer is responsible for correct classification; use a customs broker or advisor.

Can duties be reduced?

Trade agreements and origin rules may reduce rates; confirm eligibility and documentation.

What documents do I need?

Typically invoice, packing list and certificate of origin; confirm with your broker.

What the Spec Means for Buyers

Import duty starts with the HS classification of the finished packaging, so the RFQ must state destination port and country. The factory provides the export packing spec (flat-packed K=K cartons, palletizing optional) and product data for classification. What the factory can confirm: MOQ 500 PCS, die-cut ±1.5 mm, color ΔE ≤ 3.0, AQL 2.5 inspection, and FOB terms on request. Duty rates and landed cost are calculated from your HS code and Incoterm — confirm those with your broker or forwarder.
