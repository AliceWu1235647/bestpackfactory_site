RFQ quality improves when the buyer provides the fields that packaging engineers actually need. This guide turns a vague request into a quote-ready brief so the factory can recommend the right box, bag or pouch without back-and-forth.

RFQ checklist

  • Packaging type and use case
  • Size or product dimensions
  • Target quantity
  • Artwork or logo status
  • Material and finish preference
  • Delivery country
  • Sample and bulk timeline
  • Reference image or product link

Verification method and acceptance evidence

Method for Custom Packaging RFQ Parameter Guide: Record the approved sample revision, packed condition, lot size, inspection level, defect classes, acceptance limit, conditioning, equipment and pass/fail rule before inspection.

  • ISO 2859-1:2026 defines AQL-indexed sampling schemes and acceptance or rejection rules for lot-by-lot inspection.
  • ASTM D5276-19(2023) covers free-fall drop testing of loaded boxes, cylindrical containers, bags and sacks as packed systems.

Evidence limit: These standards define repeatable methods, not a passing result for a BestPackFactory order. Release decisions require a project-specific inspection or test report tied to the approved sample and production lot.

What the Spec Means for Buyers

An RFQ becomes quotable when it carries the factory parameters: internal size L×W×H, product weight, shipping method and stack height for boxes; size, product weight, filling condition, artwork, quantity and destination for flexible packaging. Add material, printing (flexo 1–4 colors or offset-laminated), finish and inspection level (AQL 2.5). MOQ is 500 PCS per size and artwork; color tolerance ΔE ≤ 3.0 and die-cut ±1.5 mm are the standard gates.

Frequently Asked Questions

What parameters make an RFQ quotable?

Size, product weight, shipping method, stack height, material, printing, finish, quantity and destination.

What tolerance appears on every quote?

Color ΔE ≤ 3.0 and die-cut tolerance ±1.5 mm are the standard production gates.
