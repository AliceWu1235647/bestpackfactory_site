Custom Packaging Quality Control: What to Inspect Before Shipment

Published August 27, 2026 · Buyer guide · By BestPackFactory

Quick answer

Inspect packaging against the approved sample on four fronts: appearance (print, finish, defects), dimensions (size and fit), function (folding, closing, sealing) and quantity. Agree the inspection timing with the supplier, use the approved sample as the standard, and document any deviations before shipment.

Decision table

CheckWhat to verifyCommon issues
AppearancePrint, color, finish, defectsScratches, misprints
DimensionsSize, thickness, fitOff-spec sizes
FunctionFolding, closing, sealingPoor creases or seals
QuantityCount per carton and totalShort counts

Inspect against the approved sample

The approved sample is the contract for appearance and function. Inspect production against it, not against memory or a photo.

Timing matters

Inspection during production catches issues while they can be fixed; final inspection before shipment protects the shipment. Agree the points with the supplier and, if needed, use a third-party inspection service.

Sampling must be representative

Random sampling across the run, cartons and shifts reflects the batch better than inspecting one carton. Agree the sampling plan in advance.

Document deviations and fixes

Record findings, agree on rework or acceptance criteria and confirm fixes before release. Keep inspection records with the order.

Buyer checklist

  1. Use the approved sample as standard
  2. Check appearance, dimensions, function
  3. Inspect at agreed timing
  4. Sample across cartons and shifts
  5. Document and resolve deviations

Frequently asked questions

Should I use a third-party inspector?

For large or high-value orders, independent inspection adds assurance; agree scope and standards first.

What are the key QC points?

Appearance, dimensions, function and quantity, checked against the approved sample.

Can I request photos during production?

Most suppliers provide production photos on request; combine with scheduled inspection for critical orders.

What the Spec Means for Buyers

A quality inspection plan starts with the numbers a factory can actually hold. For custom boxes: MOQ 500 PCS per size and artwork, die-cut tolerance ±1.5 mm for corrugated, dimension tolerance ±1.5 mm for paper boxes (unless stated), color tolerance ΔE ≤ 3.0 against the approved digital proof, and AQL 2.5 for major defects with random inspection before shipment. Corrugated mailers target 32 ECT equivalent, with E-flute 1.5 mm, B-flute 3.0 mm or BC-flute 6.0 mm in 3-ply or 5-ply. Compression and stacking tests are based on product weight and shipping carton design.

Frequently Asked Questions

What AQL level is used for packaging inspection?

AQL 2.5 for major defects with random inspection before shipment is the standard quality gate.

What tolerances should a buyer specify?

Die-cut tolerance ±1.5 mm for corrugated, dimension tolerance ±1.5 mm for paper boxes unless stated, and color ΔE ≤ 3.0 against the proof.
