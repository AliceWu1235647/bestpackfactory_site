Custom Gift Box Logo and Branding Techniques: Foil, Emboss, UV Print

Published August 21, 2026 · Buyer guide · By BestPackFactory

Quick answer

Match the technique to the surface: hot-foil stamping for metallic logos on matte or soft-touch stock, emboss or deboss for tactile depth, spot UV for subtle gloss contrast, and silk-screen for solid opaque areas on dark or textured paper. Budget roughly: foil and emboss add 5-15% to unit cost and 2-4 days of lead time; spot UV about 3-8%. Keep fine lines above 0.5 mm and confirm technique compatibility with your wrap paper.

Hot-foil stamping: the premium default

Foil transfers a thin metallic layer (gold, silver, rose gold, holographic) onto the wrap paper, and works best on smooth matte or soft-touch surfaces. Use vector artwork with a minimum line weight of 0.5 mm and avoid serif text below 6 pt. Foil cannot reproduce fine photographic detail, so keep logos and monograms simple. Confirm the foil brand and color number in the proof stage.

Emboss and deboss for tactile depth

Embossing raises the logo, debossing presses it in; both work best on uncoated or lightly coated paper at least 128 gsm. Combined with foil (registered emboss plus foil) it is the most premium common finish but adds 2-4 days and 5-15% cost. Deboss alone is the subtler, lower-cost option. Ask for a physical proof because depth reads differently on screen.

Spot UV and silk-screen

Spot UV adds a gloss layer over selected areas of a matte or laminated surface, creating contrast without metal; it is the budget-friendly premium finish at 3-8% extra. Silk-screen printing lays opaque ink, ideal for white or metallic solids on dark or textured wrap papers where CMYK cannot reach full opacity. Both need vector masks and clear zone definitions in the artwork file.

How to spec finishes in an RFQ

List every finish on a separate line with its surface (matte, soft-touch, uncoated), artwork zone and color reference. Ask whether finishes are applied before or after lamination, since order affects gloss and adhesion. Request a single combined sample with all finishes on your exact paper before approving bulk, and keep artwork layers separate (print, foil, emboss, UV) so changes do not ripple.

Buyer checklist

  1. Match technique to surface: foil on smooth matte, emboss on uncoated 128+ gsm
  2. Keep logo vectors at ≥0.5 mm line weight and avoid foil serif text under 6 pt
  3. Separate artwork layers: print, foil, emboss, UV masks
  4. Ask about finish order relative to lamination in the RFQ
  5. Approve one combined sample with all finishes on the exact wrap paper

Frequently asked questions

Which logo technique looks most premium?

Registered foil plus emboss on a matte or soft-touch surface is the classic premium combination. It adds 5-15% cost and 2-4 days of lead time, so reserve it for hero SKUs.

Can foil stamping reproduce small text?

Foil needs at least 0.5 mm line weight and works poorly below roughly 6 pt serif. Keep monograms and logos bold; fine print should be printed, not foiled.

What is the cheapest premium finish?

Spot UV on a matte or laminated surface delivers gloss contrast at about 3-8% added cost and 1-2 days of lead time, making it the most economical premium option.

What the Spec Means for Buyers

Logo techniques on rigid boxes are a finishing stack. Hot foil (die tolerance ±0.3 mm) gives metallic logos; embossing or debossing adds depth at controlled height; spot UV creates contrast on matte lamination; soft-touch film sets the base feel. The box substrate is 1200/1500/1800 gsm greyboard wrapped in 157 gsm C1S art paper, 120 gsm specialty or kraft, printed offset 1–6 colors with color ΔE ≤ 3.0. All techniques are confirmed against the approved sample before mass production at MOQ 500 PCS.

Foil stamping die tolerance is ±0.3 mm.
