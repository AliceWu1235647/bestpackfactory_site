Custom Packaging Supplier Audit Checklist: What to Verify Before You Commit

Published August 28, 2026 · Buyer guide · By BestPackFactory

Quick answer

Before committing, verify the supplier on five fronts: real production capability (equipment and shop floor), QC process (inspections and standards), capacity (current and future), communication (responsiveness and technical depth) and documentation (certificates, records, samples). A video tour or on-site audit beats a beautiful website.

Verify production is real

Ask for a live video tour or on-site audit showing printing presses, cutting machines and finishing lines. Stock photos and generic videos prove nothing.

Understand the QC process

Ask where inspections happen (incoming material, print check, final), what standards apply, and whether there are quality records you can review.

Capacity must match your plans

Confirm line capacity, current load and typical lead times for your product. A capable factory that is overbooked still misses your deadline.

Documentation completes the picture

Certificates, material declarations, inspection records and approved samples should exist and be shareable. Unverifiable claims are a reason to look further.

Buyer checklist

  1. Watch a live production tour
  2. Review the QC process
  3. Confirm capacity and lead times
  4. Test communication and expertise
  5. Verify documentation

Frequently asked questions

Is a video tour enough?

A live video tour is a good start; consider a third-party audit for large or critical orders.

What certificates matter?

Confirm certificates relevant to your product and market, and verify they are current.

How do I test communication?

Ask technical questions; a supplier who answers precisely and promptly will be easier to work with.

What the Spec Means for Buyers

Audit a packaging supplier against numbers, not promises. Confirm MOQ 500 PCS per size and artwork; die-cut tolerance ±1.5 mm for corrugated and ±1.5 mm for paper boxes unless stated; color tolerance ΔE ≤ 3.0 against the approved digital proof; AQL 2.5 for major defects with random pre-shipment inspection. Ask whether corrugated is E/B/BC flute at 3-ply or 5-ply with a 32 ECT equivalent target, whether compression tests are run from stacking height and product weight, and whether export packing is flat-packed in K=K cartons with palletizing available. Request the RFQ checklist fields: size, weight, filling condition, artwork, quantity, destination.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the single most important tolerance to verify?

Color tolerance ΔE ≤ 3.0 against the approved digital proof, because it is the most common cause of rejected packaging.

What inspection level should the audit require?

AQL 2.5 for major defects with random inspection before shipment is the industry-standard gate.
