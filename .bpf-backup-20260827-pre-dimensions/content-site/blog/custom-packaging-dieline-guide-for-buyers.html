How to Read a Packaging Dieline: A Buyer Guide to Cut Lines, Creases and Bleed

Published August 26, 2026 · Buyer guide · By BestPackFactory

Quick answer

A dieline is the technical map of your package: cut lines show where the board is cut, crease lines show where it folds, and bleed extends artwork past the cut so no white edge appears. Check the safe zone (keep text and logos inside it), confirm the dieline matches the final dimensions, and approve it before sending artwork.

Learn the three line types

Cut lines, crease lines and bleed boundaries each do a different job. Approving artwork without understanding them causes white edges, cracked folds or clipped logos in production. Ask the supplier for a line-keyed dieline (a legend showing which line is which).

Check the safe zone first

Text, logos and barcodes belong inside the safe zone, away from cut and crease risk. A logo touching the crease may fold, crack or distort. Confirm the safe zone with the supplier before production.

Verify dimensions against the product

The dieline must match the packed product: internal dimensions, thickness and closure. A dieline that looks correct but is 2mm short changes the box fit. Confirm the dieline against your product before approving.

Approve in the right format

Approve the dieline with the print file together, in the correct color mode and resolution. Keep the approved dieline and artwork with the order so production and reorders stay consistent.

Buyer checklist

  1. Identify cut, crease and bleed lines
  2. Keep content inside the safe zone
  3. Verify dimensions against product
  4. Approve dieline and artwork together
  5. Keep approved files for reorders

Frequently asked questions

What happens if my artwork has no bleed?

You may see white edges after cutting; extend artwork past the cut line as the supplier specifies.

What is a safe zone?

The area inside the cut and crease lines where critical content must stay to avoid clipping or distortion.

Who provides the dieline?

The supplier provides the production dieline; confirm it matches your product before artwork approval.

What the Spec Means for Buyers

Reading a dieline means tracking five elements: cut line (final shape), crease line (fold), glue area (assembly), bleed (3 mm print extension) and safety margin (keep text inside). The factory confirms the dieline from your product dimensions on request; die-cut tolerance is ±1.5 mm for corrugated and ±1.5 mm for paper boxes unless stated. Color is matched at ΔE ≤ 3.0.
