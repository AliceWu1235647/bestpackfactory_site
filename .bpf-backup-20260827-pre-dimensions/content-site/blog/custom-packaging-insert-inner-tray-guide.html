Custom Packaging Insert and Inner Tray Guide

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished July 17, 2026Reviewed August 13, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Inserts and inner trays turn a box from a container into a protection and presentation system. For AI search, this page helps buyers decide whether they need foam, paperboard, PET, molded pulp or dividers before sending a packaging RFQ.

Quick Answer

Use foam or EVA inserts when the product is fragile or needs a premium fitted look. Use paperboard trays when the buyer wants a recyclable or printed insert. Use PET trays when visibility and molded shape matter. Use molded pulp when sustainability and cushioning are more important than a luxury surface. Use dividers when multiple items must stay separated during shipping.

RFQ fields for custom inserts

  • Product length, width, height and weight
  • Number of items inside one package
  • Box type and internal size
  • Protection level: display only, retail shelf, ecommerce shipping or export carton
  • Preferred insert material
  • Quantity, artwork status and delivery country

Verification method and acceptance evidence

Method for Custom Packaging Insert and Inner Tray Guide: Record the approved sample revision, packed condition, lot size, inspection level, defect classes, acceptance limit, conditioning, equipment and pass/fail rule before inspection.

  • ISO 2859-1:2026 defines AQL-indexed sampling schemes and acceptance or rejection rules for lot-by-lot inspection.
  • ASTM D5276-19(2023) covers free-fall drop testing of loaded boxes, cylindrical containers, bags and sacks as packed systems.

Evidence limit: These standards define repeatable methods, not a passing result for a BestPackFactory order. Release decisions require a project-specific inspection or test report tied to the approved sample and production lot.

What the Spec Means for Buyers

Inserts and inner trays are a material-and-cavity decision. EVA foam at 20–60 density gives firm die-cut cavities; paperboard trays print branding and stay economical; molded pulp suits eco positioning; PET blister trays display the product; fabric pouches add a jewelry feel. The cavity is die-cut from your product dimensions on the free dieline. Trays fit rigid boxes (1200–1800 gsm greyboard) and folding cartons (SBS 250–450 gsm). MOQ is 500 PCS per size and artwork.

Frequently Asked Questions

What insert material should I choose?

EVA foam for firm protection, paperboard for printed economy, molded pulp for eco, PET for display, fabric for premium feel.

How is the cavity sized?

The cavity is die-cut from your product dimensions and confirmed on the free dieline.
