Paper Bowl and Sauce Cup Sizes: Rim Diameters and PP vs PET vs Paper Lids

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished October 30, 2026Reviewed October 30, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Quick answer

Pick the bowl from the portion you serve, then lock its rim diameter, because the rim decides which lids fit. Use PP lids for hot soups and anything reheated, PET lids for cold dishes where a crystal-clear view sells the food, and paper lids for hot food when you want less plastic and a printable top. For sauce cups, 1 oz suits dips, 2 oz suits dressings and most sides of sauce, and 4 oz suits larger sides. Sizes and rims vary between moulds, so confirm the fit on a physical sample before you order bowls and lids together.

Typical paper bowl sizes and rims

These are common market sizes, not a fixed standard. Rim diameter depends on the mould, and the same capacity is often made both as a tall soup container and as a wide, shallow bowl. Treat the rims as a starting point and confirm on a sample.

One US fluid ounce is about 29.6 ml. Ask whether a stated capacity is the brim-full volume or the intended fill, and fill a sample with your real portion, including broth and toppings.

Tall or wide?

Tall, narrow containers keep soup hotter for longer and slosh less in delivery bags. Wide, shallow bowls show off salads, poke and toppings, and are easier to eat from with a fork. Many menus use a shared rim across two or three heights so that one lid size covers several portions; if you can, plan your range around a common rim.

Recycled PET and plant-based lid materials also exist; ask about their temperature limits before use. The inner coating of the bowl matters as much as the lid; our PE vs PLA vs water-based coating answer compares the options.

Decision rules by food

  • Hot liquid (soup, broth, curry): tall bowl with a PP lid, vented if the food is very hot, or a paper lid with a tight fit for short holds.
  • Hot dry food (rice, stir-fry, noodles): paper lid or PP lid; a paper lid gives you a large print area.
  • Cold display food (salad, poke, fruit): wide bowl with a PET lid.
  • Delivery with longer transit: choose the tightest-fitting lid and add a tamper-evident seal sticker.

Sauce cup sizes by use

Paper sauce cups (also called portion or condiment cups) cover small sides. Paper versions often share one rim across several sizes and change height instead, while plastic portion cups more commonly step up in diameter. Confirm the rim before ordering lids.

  2. Holding and delivery time, and whether customers reheat the food.
  3. Bowl shape (tall or wide), lid type and any existing lids you want to keep using.

Common mistakes

  • Ordering lids by bowl ounces instead of by measured rim diameter.
  • Using PET lids for hot soup.
  • Choosing a 1 oz cup for a dressing that is meant to cover a whole salad.
  • Skipping a hot-fill and delivery test with real food.

Frequently asked questions

Which lid is best for hot soup in a paper bowl?

A PP lid is the common choice for hot soup because it tolerates heat; vented versions release steam. A tight-fitting paper lid also works for hot food and reduces plastic. PET lids are generally for cold food only.

Do all 16 oz paper bowls take the same lid?

No. Lid fit is set by rim diameter, which depends on the mould. A 16 oz bowl may be tall with a rim near 98 mm or wide with a rim near 115 mm, so measure the rim or test a sample.

What size sauce cups do restaurants use?

1 oz cups are common for dips such as ketchup, 2 oz for dressings and salsa, and 3 to 4 oz for larger sides and shared dips. Choose by how much sauce covers the dish.
