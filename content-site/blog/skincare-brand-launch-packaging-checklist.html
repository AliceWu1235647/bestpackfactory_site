Skincare Brand Launch Packaging Checklist: Every Layer You Need to Order

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished October 30, 2026Reviewed October 30, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Quick answer

A typical skincare launch needs up to six packaging layers per product: the primary container (bottle, jar, tube or dropper), a label or shrink sleeve on it, a printed folding carton, an insert if the container can move, a shipping mailer for online orders, and an optional printed card. Lock the container first, because every other layer is sized from it. Write the regulatory copy before artwork starts, because US and EU rules decide what must appear on the carton the shopper sees. At BestPackFactory every printed layer is made in our own factory, with an MOQ of 500 PCS per design.

Order your decisions in this sequence

  1. Freeze the primary container. Get a physical sample of the exact bottle or jar with its closure. Cartons and inserts are sized from the container, so a late change to a different pump height can make printed cartons unusable.
  2. Choose the decoration method. Film labels (PP or PE) are commonly chosen for products used with wet hands or oils, because paper labels can wrinkle or stain. Shrink sleeves suit contoured bottles and full 360-degree graphics. Paper labels work well on dry-use jars and for a natural look.
  3. Size the carton around the closed container. Measure the tallest point (pump, dropper bulb, cap) and the widest point, then let the supplier add clearance. Our skincare box insert fit guide covers the tolerance question in detail, so it is not repeated here.
  4. Add an insert only where movement is a real risk. A snug carton can be enough for a plastic tube; glass droppers and gift sets usually need one.
  5. Size the mailer for your common order. Most launch brands plan for one, two or three units per parcel. One mailer size that fits the most common order is easier to stock than several.
  6. Do cards and extras last. They depend on the final mailer and carton size.

Outer carton label checklist: US and EU

This is a general checklist, not legal advice. Your responsible person, regulatory consultant or importer should confirm the final text for each market. Print suppliers check that artwork fits the dieline; they do not certify cosmetic compliance.

United States (FD&C Act, Fair Packaging and Labeling Act, MoCRA)

  • Statement of identity (what the product is) on the principal display panel.
  • Net quantity of contents on the principal display panel; metric units are commonly shown alongside US units.
  • Name and place of business of the manufacturer, packer or distributor.
  • Ingredient declaration, generally in descending order of predominance, on the outer container when the product is sold in a carton.
  • Any warning statements that apply to the product type.
  • Under the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 (MoCRA), a domestic US address, phone number or electronic contact such as a website through which adverse events can be reported.
  • Fragrance allergen labeling is the subject of FDA rulemaking under MoCRA; check the current status before you finalise artwork.

European Union (Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009)

  • Name and address of the EU responsible person.
  • Country of origin for products imported into the EU.
  • Nominal content by weight or volume.
  • Date of minimum durability where durability is 30 months or less, or the period-after-opening (open jar) symbol where it is longer.
  • Precautions for use, a batch number or reference, and the product function unless it is clear from the presentation.
  • The ingredient list, which may appear on the outer packaging alone.
  • Text in the language required by each member state where the product is sold.

Several items must appear on both the container and the carton. Where a container is too small, EU rules allow certain information on an enclosed leaflet, label or tag, signposted on the pack with the hand-in-book symbol. Plan that early: it changes how many printed pieces you order.

Decision rules

  • Use a carton when you sell through retail, sell gift sets, use glass, or the container cannot carry all mandatory text legibly.
  • Skip the carton when you sell only online, the container is sturdy plastic, the label carries all required text, and the mailer plus insert protects the product. This is a common way to reduce cost and waste at launch.
  • Choose a shrink sleeve over a label for curved or unusual bottle shapes and full-coverage designs; choose labels for flat panels and when copy is likely to change after the first run.
  • Print the mailer outside when unboxing content is part of your marketing; print only the inside, or use a sticker, when parcels travel through marketplaces or you prefer discreet shipping.

Planning around MOQ 500 PCS per design

Each different artwork is a separate design, so three SKUs with their own cartons and labels mean six designs of 500 PCS or more each. To keep the first order manageable, brands commonly use one carton structure and size across SKUs of the same container, change only colour and text per SKU, share one mailer and one card design across the range, and keep claims that may change (such as reviews or awards) off the printed carton.

What to send in your RFQ

  1. A sample or drawing of each container with closure, and the measured height and diameter.
  2. Which layers you need per SKU (label, sleeve, carton, insert, mailer, card) and quantity per design.
  3. Sales channels and target markets, so the carton panel layout leaves room for the right text.
  4. Artwork status, dielines needed, finishes you are considering and any reference packs.
  5. Delivery address or Incoterm and your target launch date.

Common mistakes

  • Ordering cartons before the final pump or dropper is confirmed.
  • Designing the front panel first and discovering later that ingredients and warnings do not fit.
  • Using paper labels on products used in the shower.
  • Making a separate mailer per SKU when one size would serve every order.
  • Printing compliance contact details that are not yet set up, such as a US contact for adverse event reports.

Frequently asked questions

What packaging does a new skincare brand need to launch?

Usually a primary container, a label or shrink sleeve, a printed folding carton, an insert where the container can move, a shipping mailer for online orders and an optional card. Online-only brands with sturdy containers sometimes skip the carton.

Do I need a box for every skincare product?

No. A carton is mainly needed for retail, gifting, glass containers or when the container is too small to carry all required text. For online-only plastic containers, a label plus a protective mailer can be enough.

What has to be printed on a skincare box for the EU?

Under Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 the outer packaging commonly needs the responsible person, country of origin for imports, nominal content, durability date or period-after-opening symbol, precautions, batch number, function and the ingredient list. Confirm final text with your responsible person.
