Working with a China Packaging Factory: Communication, Time Zones and Remote Proof Approval

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished October 30, 2026Reviewed October 30, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Quick answer

China runs on a single time zone, UTC+8, so plan for your messages to be read during the factory's next working day and use the short overlap window for calls. Use WhatsApp or WeChat for quick questions, photos and video, but put every specification, price, proof approval and change in email or a signed document. Share print-ready vector files with the dieline, approve versioned proofs in writing, and use a physical sample for anything colour-critical.

Time zones: find your overlap window

Mainland China uses China Standard Time (UTC+8) everywhere and does not observe daylight saving time. Your gap to the factory therefore shifts by an hour when your own country changes its clocks.

For buyers in the Americas, the most efficient rhythm is usually to send a complete, clear message at the end of your day so the factory can act on it during theirs. One vague message can cost a full day per round trip, so ask all your questions at once and attach the files they relate to.

What to confirm in writing

  1. Specification: style, inner dimensions, material and thickness, print colours, finishes, inserts.
  2. Quantity and tolerance: order quantity and whether a small over- or under-run is accepted.
  3. Price and terms: unit price, setup or tooling charges, Incoterm, currency and payment terms.
  4. Timeline: sample date, production start, ship date, and what happens if approval is late.
  5. Proof approval: which file version you approved and on which date.
  6. Packing and labelling: units per carton, carton marks, pallet requirements.
  7. Changes: any change after approval, with its effect on price and date.

Number every artwork and proof file (for example _v3) and refer to that number in each approval. Most remote-ordering disputes trace back to someone approving or printing the wrong version. For a full RFQ field list, see the custom packaging RFQ checklist.

Sharing artwork files

  • Send vector print files (PDF or AI) placed on the factory's dieline, with fonts outlined and images embedded at print resolution.
  • List spot colours by Pantone reference and say which elements are foil, embossing or spot UV, on separate layers where possible.
  • Large files often exceed email attachment limits. Some cloud-storage services common outside China are not reliably accessible from mainland China, so ask which link type the factory can open before sending.
  • Keep one master folder and send the complete file set each time, rather than single corrected elements.

Approving proofs and samples remotely

  1. Digital proof: check text, barcodes, panel positions and the dieline overlay. This confirms layout, not colour.
  2. White sample: ask for photos and a short video showing the unprinted box assembled, opened and closed with your product inside.
  3. Printed sample: request photos in daylight next to a Pantone reference, and for colour-critical work have the sample couriered to you.
  4. Written sign-off: reply by email naming the version and any remaining corrections.
  5. Pre-shipment check: ask for photos of finished goods, carton marks and packed cartons before the balance payment.

The differences between proof types are explained in digital proof vs white sample vs press proof.

Plan around Chinese holidays

The biggest interruption is Chinese New Year (Spring Festival), which falls between late January and mid-February. Many factories close for longer than the official holiday because workers travel home, and production often slows in the weeks before and after. The National Day holiday from 1 October and the Labour Day holiday around 1 May also stop production for several days, and shorter holidays such as Qingming, Dragon Boat and Mid-Autumn Festival can affect schedules. The government sometimes sets weekend make-up working days around holidays, so ask the factory for its exact closure dates each year and approve artwork well before them.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a chat "OK" as a confirmed order.
  • Sending corrections across several apps so nobody has the full picture.
  • Approving colour from a phone screen.
  • Leaving artwork approval until just before a holiday closure.
  • Skipping supplier checks; see how to verify a China factory is legitimate before paying a deposit.

Frequently asked questions

What time zone are Chinese packaging factories in?

All of mainland China uses China Standard Time, UTC+8, with no daylight saving time. The gap to your office therefore changes when your own country switches clocks.

Should I use WhatsApp, WeChat or email with a Chinese factory?

Use chat (WhatsApp or WeChat, whichever the factory's export team actively monitors) for quick questions, photos and videos, and use email for anything that defines the order: specifications, quotations, proofs, approvals and changes. Then confirm chat decisions by email.

How do I approve a packaging proof without visiting the factory?

Check a versioned PDF proof against the dieline, then ask for photos or video of the white sample and printed sample, and for colour-critical jobs have a physical sample couriered to you. Approve in writing, quoting the proof version number.

When do Chinese factories close for holidays?

The longest closure is around Chinese New Year, which falls between late January and mid-February. The National Day holiday in early October and the Labour Day holiday around 1 May also stop or slow production. Ask the factory for its own closure dates each year.
