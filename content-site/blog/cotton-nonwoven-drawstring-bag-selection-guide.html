Cotton Tote vs Non-Woven Bag vs Drawstring Pouch: Which Promotional or Retail Bag to Choose

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished October 30, 2026Reviewed October 30, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Quick answer

Choose a cotton tote when the bag should feel premium, be washed and be reused for a long time. Choose a non-woven PP bag when you need a low-cost, lightweight carrier for events or retail with full-colour print. Choose a drawstring pouch when the bag holds small items or is worn on the back. Decide the material weight, print method and handle from the load the bag must carry, then confirm everything on a printed sample.

Material weight: what the gsm number tells you

Fabric weight is the best single indicator of how a bag will feel and how much it can carry. Cotton is quoted in grams per square metre (gsm) or in ounces per square yard; one oz per square yard is roughly 34 gsm. In the market, light promotional cotton totes are commonly somewhere around 120–180 gsm (roughly 4–5 oz), everyday canvas totes around 270 gsm (about 8 oz), and heavy canvas well above that. Non-woven PP bags are commonly made from sheet in the region of 60–120 gsm, with 70–80 gsm a frequent choice for shopping bags. Check that an oz figure means ounces per square yard; some fabrics are described per linear yard, which is not the same.

These ranges are general market references, not a promise of what any one factory stocks. Ask for the fabric weight in writing on the quotation, and feel a sample before ordering: two fabrics with the same gsm can differ in weave, stiffness and print surface.

Printing: match the method to the artwork

  • Screen printing is the common choice for one to a few spot colours on cotton and non-woven. It is durable and economical for logos and simple graphics.
  • Heat transfer is used for full-colour images and gradients on fabric. Ask how the transfer behaves after washing.
  • Laminated non-woven carries a printed film on the outside, which allows photographic, full-coverage designs and a glossy or matte look. Lamination adds stiffness and makes the bag less fabric-like.
  • Fabric colour matters. Printing light inks on dark fabric typically needs an underbase, which affects cost and feel.

Fabric does not reproduce fine lines and small text as sharply as paper. Keep minimum line widths generous and check the printed sample at arm's length, the way customers will see it.

Load and construction

How much a bag can carry depends less on the fabric alone and more on construction: handle length and width, how the handle is attached (sewn, cross-stitched or heat-welded on non-woven), whether there is a gusset or flat bottom, and stitch density. If your bag must carry wine bottles, books or groceries, state the target load in kilograms and ask for the sample to be loaded with that weight and carried. Load capacity for a specific bag is confirmed in the quotation and on the sample, not assumed from a material name.

Handle choice matters too: long handles (shoulder carry) suit totes for daily use; short handles suit retail carriers; drawstring cords double as straps on backpack styles but cut into the shoulder under heavy loads.

Washability and care

Cotton is generally machine washable, but untreated cotton can shrink, and some prints fade with repeated washing. If the bag is merchandise meant to be washed, ask about shrinkage and print durability and wash a sample yourself. Non-woven PP is heat-sensitive: it can deform with hot water, tumble drying or ironing, so it is usually wiped clean or hand washed. Add care instructions if customers will keep the bag.

A note on environmental claims

Non-woven PP is a plastic material (polypropylene), so it is not plastic-free, and a laminated version adds another plastic layer. Cotton is a natural fibre, but how any bag compares environmentally depends on how many times it is reused and on the method of the study. Avoid printing environmental claims on a bag unless you can support them for your market.

Decision rules

  • Choose cotton when the bag is part of your brand, will be sold or gifted as merchandise, or must survive washing.
  • Choose non-woven when you need many bags at a low unit cost, a structured shape with a gusset, or full-colour print on a budget.
  • Choose a drawstring pouch for small products, dust bags, sample kits or event backpacks.
  • Choose a paper bag instead when the bag is a single-trip retail carrier and you want a printed paper look; see paper bags and custom paper bags wholesale.

What to send in your RFQ

  1. Bag type and size (width × height × gusset), handle type and length.
  2. Preferred material and fabric weight, or the feel you want and a reference bag.
  3. Fabric colour and print colours; artwork as vector files; print area on one or both sides.
  4. Target load in kilograms and what the bag will carry.
  5. Whether the bag must be washable, and any care label.
  6. Quantity, packing, destination and target date.

Common mistakes

  • Ordering by gsm alone without feeling a sample.
  • Putting fine text or thin lines on textured fabric.
  • Choosing short handles for a bag meant to be carried on the shoulder.
  • Assuming non-woven bags can be ironed or hot-washed.
  • Printing unsupported eco claims on the bag.

Frequently asked questions

Is a non-woven bag plastic-free?

No. Non-woven bags are commonly made from polypropylene, which is a plastic, and laminated versions add a plastic film. Do not describe them as plastic-free.

What gsm should a cotton tote be?

Light promotional cotton totes are commonly around 120 to 180 gsm, while heavier canvas totes are well above that. Choose by the feel and load you need, and confirm on a sample.

Can non-woven bags be washed?

They are usually wiped clean or gently hand washed. Polypropylene is heat-sensitive, so hot washing, tumble drying and ironing can deform the bag.

Which bag can carry the most weight?

Load depends mainly on construction: handle attachment, stitching, gusset and fabric weight. State your target load in kilograms and test it on a sample.
