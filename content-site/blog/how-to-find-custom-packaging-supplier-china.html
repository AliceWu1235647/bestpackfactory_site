How to Find a Reliable Custom Packaging Supplier in China: B2B Guide 2025

Published June 2025 · 10 min read · By BestPackFactory

China manufactures over 40% of the world's packaging. Finding a reliable supplier is essential for B2B buyers who want competitive pricing, consistent quality, and a long-term partnership. This guide walks you through every step — from initial sourcing to placing your first bulk order.

1. Where to Find Packaging Suppliers in China

Alibaba.com

The world's largest B2B sourcing platform. Filter by "Verified Supplier", "Gold Supplier", and trade assurance eligibility. Look for suppliers with 5+ years on the platform and at least 50+ completed transactions.

Global Sources

More focused on mid-to-large buyers. Suppliers tend to be more established. Good for buyers with higher volume requirements.

Canton Fair

The world's largest trade fair, held twice yearly in Guangzhou. Ideal for face-to-face supplier meetings and product sampling. Spring session: April/May. Autumn session: October/November.

Direct Web Search (Google, Bing)

Many established factories maintain professional English-language websites. Search terms like "custom flexible packaging manufacturer China MOQ 500" or "B2B coffee bag supplier factory direct" can surface suppliers who invest in their online presence — often a positive signal.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • Unable to provide physical samples (claims samples take 30+ days)
  • No verifiable factory address or reluctance to video call from factory floor
  • Pricing that is significantly below market (often means material substitution risk)
  • No written quality standards or refusal to sign quality agreement
  • Communication only through personal WeChat with no company email

3. The Sample Testing Process

Never skip the sample stage. A proper sample process includes:

  1. Spec sheet confirmation: Supplier confirms all material and dimension specs in writing
  2. Pre-production sample (PPS): Printed sample using your actual artwork — review color, size, zipper function
  3. Material testing: Send samples to a third-party lab for oxygen transmission rate (OTR), moisture vapor transmission rate (MVTR), and seal strength testing if required
  4. Abuse testing: Drop test, compression test, and temperature cycle test where applicable

4. Negotiating Your First Order

Key terms to negotiate:

  • MOQ: Push for 500–1,000 PCS on your first order to minimize risk
  • Payment terms: 30/70 (30% deposit, 70% before shipment) is standard for first-time buyers
  • Lead time: Get lead times in writing with penalty clauses for delay
  • Defect policy: Agree on AQL standard (2.5 is typical) and replacement policy for defective units
  • Incoterm: FOB is recommended for experienced importers; DDP for simplicity if your supplier offers it

5. Quality Control During Production

For orders above 5,000 PCS, consider hiring a third-party QC inspector (SGS, Bureau Veritas, QIMA) to conduct an in-line inspection before shipment. Cost is typically $200–$400 per man-day and can save significant losses from defective shipments.
