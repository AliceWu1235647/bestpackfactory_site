PET Window Patch vs Open Die-Cut Window: Which Should Your Paper Box Use?

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished October 30, 2026Reviewed October 30, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Quick answer

Use a film window patch when the product is unwrapped food, needs protection from dust and fingers, or must not fall out; PET is the common default because it is clear, stiff and widely accepted for food contact when a suitable grade is specified. Choose anti-fog PET for chilled food that can mist up. Use an open die-cut window, with no film, when the product is already individually sealed or wrapped, when customers should touch or smell it, or when you want a mono-material paper box. Keep the window away from folds and glue flaps and confirm the layout on the dieline.

Choose a film window patch when…

  • The food is unwrapped. Donuts, cupcakes, cookies and pastries in a bakery box need a covered opening for hygiene and to stop the product drying out quickly.
  • The product is small or loose and could fall through an open window.
  • The box is handled a lot on shelf and fingerprints or dust on the product would be a problem.
  • The box will be refrigerated: use anti-fog film so customers can still see the product after condensation forms.

For bakery formats and dimensions, see our bakery box size guide and bakery and donut boxes.

Choose an open die-cut window when…

  • The product already sits in its own sealed wrapper, jar or bottle.
  • Touch or scent is part of the sale, for example soap or textiles.
  • You want the carton to be paper only, which is simpler to explain to customers and recyclers.
  • The window is small and functional, such as a thumb cut or a peek-through shape that shows a colour or logo.

Grease, moisture and food contact

The film itself does not absorb grease, but the board around the window does. Oily or buttery products need a grease-resistant board or coating on the inside, otherwise stains spread from the window edge. If the film can touch food, ask for food-contact compliance documents for the film and adhesive that match your market, for example under EU plastics rules (Regulation (EU) No 10/2011) or US FDA requirements. Our food packaging compliance document checklist lists what to request.

Recyclability and the EU PPWR

In paper recycling, a small plastic window is usually separated out as reject material during pulping, but it is still a non-fibre component, and some recycling schemes, retailers or brand policies prefer paper-only packaging. In the EU, the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2025/40) applies from 12 August 2026, with recyclability performance grades based on design-for-recycling criteria phased in from 2030 through Commission acts. If recyclability claims or EPR fees matter for your product, check the current guidance in each market before choosing a film window, and consider an open window or no window at all.

Design rules for the window

  1. Keep a solid board margin between the window and any crease, glue flap or cut edge; a margin of around 10 mm or more is common, but confirm it on the dieline with the factory.
  2. Let the film overlap the opening by several millimetres on each side so the glue sits on flat board, not on a fold.
  3. Do not run a flat patch across a fold unless the structure is designed for it; it complicates gluing and folding.
  4. Round the window corners where possible; sharp inside corners are more likely to tear.
  5. Size the window to the product, not the panel. Very large windows weaken the panel and show any movement inside, so consider an insert if the product can shift.
  6. Plan the print around it. Barcodes, legal text and folding tabs must stay on solid board. Use a tuck end box dieline or your supplier's template to check positions.

What to put in your RFQ

  • Window size, shape and position on the dieline, or ask the supplier to propose it.
  • Film type (no film, PET, anti-fog PET or a plant-based film) and whether it will touch food.
  • Storage conditions: ambient, chilled or frozen.
  • Board grade and any grease-resistant coating; see the board options for custom folding cartons.
  • Recyclability or compostability claims you intend to print, so the material choice can support them.

Common mistakes

  • Standard PET on a chilled cake box, so the window fogs over in the fridge.
  • A window drawn too close to a crease, so the box cracks or the film lifts when folded.
  • Choosing an open window for unwrapped food.
  • Printing a "plastic-free" or "100% recyclable" claim on a box that has a film window.
  • No grease barrier around the window on oily products.

Frequently asked questions

Should a bakery box have an open window or a PET window?

For unwrapped baked goods, a film window is the usual choice because it keeps out dust and handling while showing the product. Use anti-fog PET if the box will be refrigerated. Open windows suit products that are already individually wrapped.

Is PET or PVC better for box windows?

PET is the common choice for new designs: it is clear, stiff and available in food-contact grades. Many brands and retailers avoid PVC for environmental and recycling reasons, so check your market and customer requirements.

Can a paper box with a plastic window be recycled?

In paper recycling a small plastic window is usually separated out during pulping, but it is still a non-fibre component and some schemes and retailers prefer paper-only packaging. Check the current guidance in each market, including the EU PPWR, before making recyclability claims.

How far should a box window be from a fold?

Keep a solid board margin between the window and any crease, glue flap or edge. Around 10 mm or more is common, but the exact margin should be confirmed on the dieline for your box style and board.
