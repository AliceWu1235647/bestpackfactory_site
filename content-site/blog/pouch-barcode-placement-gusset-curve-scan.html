Where to Put the Barcode on a Stand-Up Pouch So It Scans After Filling

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished October 30, 2026Reviewed October 30, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Quick answer

Put the barcode in the flattest part of the back panel, roughly centred left to right and in the middle-to-lower area that stays smooth once the pouch is filled, well clear of the side seals, the bottom gusset fold and the zipper or tear notch. If the filled panel bulges, turn the symbol to ladder orientation (bars horizontal) so the curve shortens the bars instead of hiding the ends, keep the quiet zones free of creases and seals, and print the bars in a dark colour on an opaque white patch. Then scan a filled, sealed sample before approving the print.

A barcode that verifies perfectly on a flat PDF can fail at the checkout once the pouch is filled. The panel bulges, the gusset pulls the bottom of the film into a curve, the zipper adds a ridge and the film wrinkles near the seals. This guide covers where to place the symbol on a stand-up pouch so it still reads after filling. For barcode basics such as UPC-A vs EAN-13, size and colour rules in general, see our packaging barcode requirements guide; this article only covers the pouch-specific part.

Why stand-up pouches cause scanning problems

Most retail scanners need to see the full width of the symbol, including the blank margins (quiet zones) on each side. On a pouch, several things work against that:

  • Bulge. A filled pouch is not flat. The panel bows outward, most strongly where the product settles, which is usually the lower half.
  • The bottom gusset. The base fold pulls the lower part of the front and back panels into a curve so the pouch can stand. Bars placed just above the gusset often curve away from the scanner.
  • Seals and zipper. Heat seals are thicker, glossy and sometimes rippled. A zipper adds a raised band and the area above it creases each time the pack is opened.
  • Settling. Powders settle in transit and the headspace collapses into folds.
  • Reflective or clear film. Metallised and gloss films reflect the scanner light, and clear windows give no background at all.

Placement checklist

  1. Start from the back panel. GS1 placement guidance for bags points to the centre of the back, where wrinkles are least likely.
  2. Find the flat zone on a filled pouch. Fill a plain or sample pouch with the real product at the real fill weight, stand it up and mark the area that stays flat. Use that, not the flat dieline, to position the symbol. Our stand-up pouch dieline shows the seal and gusset areas to keep clear.
  3. Keep the whole symbol off seals and folds. Bars, quiet zones and human-readable digits should sit entirely inside the panel, not touching a side seal, the gusset fold line or the zipper band. GS1 general guidance also says a symbol should be no closer than about 8 mm to the edge of the item; on a pouch, measure that from the inner edge of the seal, not from the edge of the film.
  4. Choose orientation for the curve. Picket fence orientation (bars vertical) is the common default. If the filled panel curves noticeably left to right, ladder orientation (bars horizontal, symbol running up the panel) is usually safer, because the curve then only shortens the bars instead of hiding the end bars. GS1 specifications describe this choice for curved surfaces; the decisive check is a scan on the filled pouch.
  5. Protect the quiet zones. For EAN-13 the light margin is commonly 11 modules on the left and 7 on the right; UPC-A needs 9 modules on each side. Keep those zones free of text, graphics, seal edges and creases.
  6. Do not shrink or truncate the symbol to make it fit. Retail EAN/UPC symbols are normally printed between 80% and 200% of nominal size. If space is tight, choose a larger pouch panel area rather than cutting bar height.
  7. Print dark bars on an opaque light background. Black or another very dark colour on white is the safest choice. On metallised, clear or kraft film, put a solid white ink patch behind the whole symbol including quiet zones. See our white ink underprint guide for file setup.

Decision rules

  • Choose picket fence when the filled back panel stays flat across its width, which is common on wider pouches with light or loose fills.
  • Choose ladder when the panel bulges side to side, on narrow pouches, or when the fill is dense (powder, rice, pet food) and the pouch rounds out.
  • Move the symbol up if the lower panel curves into the gusset, but stay below the zipper band and the top headspace fold.

How to test before you approve

Ask for a filled-pouch scan test as part of sampling, not just a flat proof check. A practical routine:

  1. Fill a sample to the target weight, seal it and leave it standing for a day so the product settles.
  2. Scan it with a handheld scanner and a flatbed or counter scanner if your retailer uses one, from several angles.
  3. Repeat after stacking it in a carton.
  4. If your retailer or distributor requires a verification grade, have the printed film checked with a barcode verifier against the grade they specify. A smartphone app is not a verifier.

Confirm your retailer's pass criteria before printing.

What to send in your RFQ

  • Pouch type and size, including gusset depth and zipper position.
  • Product and fill weight, so the bulge can be judged; see the pouch size by fill weight guide.
  • Barcode type (EAN-13, UPC-A or other), the number from your GS1 member organisation and the size you want printed.
  • Film surface: clear, metallised, white, kraft or matte, plus any matte or gloss varnish areas.
  • Your retailer's scanning or verification requirement, if any.
  • A request for a filled-pouch scan check on the sample.

Common mistakes

  • Placing the barcode by eye on the flat dieline, low on the back panel, where it later curves into the gusset.
  • Letting the quiet zone run into the side seal or the zipper band.
  • Printing bars directly on metallised or clear film with no white patch.
  • Reducing bar height to fit a busy back panel.
  • Approving the barcode from a flat proof without ever scanning a filled, sealed pouch.

Frequently asked questions

Where should the barcode go on a stand-up pouch?

In most cases on the back panel, roughly centred, in the area that stays flat after filling, clear of the side seals, the bottom gusset fold and the zipper or tear notch.

Should a pouch barcode be vertical or horizontal?

Bars vertical (picket fence) is the usual default. If the filled panel curves side to side, bars horizontal (ladder orientation) is usually safer because the curve only shortens the bars. Confirm by scanning a filled sample.

Can I print a barcode directly on a metallised or clear pouch?

It is not recommended. Print the bars in a dark colour on a solid white ink patch that also covers the quiet zones, so the scanner sees a consistent light background.

How do I check that the barcode will scan after filling?

Fill a sample to the target weight, seal it, let the product settle, then scan it from several angles with the scanner types your retailer uses. If a verification grade is required, use a barcode verifier.
