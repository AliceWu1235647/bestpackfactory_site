Custom Packaging for Japanese Brands: Setting Quality Expectations with a China Factory

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished October 30, 2026Reviewed October 30, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Quick answer

Japanese brands usually judge packaging on visible finish as much as on function, so put the quality expectation in writing: an inspection standard with photographed limit samples, an AQL sampling plan (JIS Z 9015-1 is aligned with ISO 2859-1), a colour reference such as a DIC or Pantone swatch approved on the real substrate, Japanese recycling identification marks where required, and carton marking rules for the receiving warehouse. Agree these before sampling, because China–Japan transit is short and approval cycles usually decide the schedule.

Why Japan needs a different brief

Many packaging problems between Japanese buyers and overseas factories are not about structure or material. They come from different ideas of what counts as a defect. A small speck under lamination, slight greyboard showing at a rigid box corner, or a faint glue mark may pass a general export inspection and still be rejected by a Japanese retailer or customer. Write an inspection standard with limit samples

In Japan it is common practice to agree a written inspection standard supported by limit samples (gendo mihon): physical or photographed examples of the worst condition that is still acceptable for each defect type. For packaging, typical entries include specks or dust under film, scratches and scuffs on soft-touch or dark solids, foil breaks or fill-in, misregistration, wrap paper wrinkles or lifting, exposed board at corners, glue marks, colour variation across a lot, and odour.

Make the document usable on the factory floor: one defect per row, a photo of acceptable and unacceptable, the panel it applies to, and the viewing condition. Viewing distance and lighting change what inspectors see; ISO 3664 describes standard viewing conditions for comparing prints. Bilingual Japanese and English text with photos avoids translation disputes.

Set an AQL plan both sides understand

Japanese buyers often refer to JIS Z 9015-1, the Japanese standard for sampling by attributes, which is aligned with ISO 2859-1. Writing the plan in terms of ISO 2859-1 (inspection level, sampling type and AQL per defect class) lets a Chinese factory or third-party inspector apply it directly. The AQL values are the buyer’s commercial decision. Many brands classify any defect on the main display face as major and set tighter limits than for inside panels. Whatever you choose, agree it before mass production, not at inspection.

Colour: DIC, Pantone and Japan Color

The DIC Color Guide is widely used in Japan, while many overseas printers work mainly from Pantone references. If your brand colour is a DIC number, confirm whether the factory will mix to DIC directly or to a nearest Pantone or custom formula, because conversions are not exact. For CMYK work, Japan Color is the national printing reference built on ISO 12647-2, which helps when your designer works to a Japan Color profile. Approve colour on the actual board and finish, since lamination and varnish shift appearance; the method is covered in the colour tolerance approval guide.

Japanese text and typesetting

Send Japanese text inside the approved artwork with fonts outlined or embedded; factories may not hold licensed Japanese fonts, and substitutions change character shapes. State whether panels are set vertically (tategaki) or horizontally. Very small kana and kanji can close up in fine print or under foil, so check legibility on the physical sample and have it proofread in Japan.

Recycling identification marks

Under Japan’s Act on the Promotion of Effective Utilization of Resources, many paper and plastic containers and packaging that households discard must carry an identification mark, such as the paper mark or the plastic mark. Corrugated cardboard is outside the paper-mark obligation and is usually marked voluntarily. The obligation generally falls on the business that uses the packaging for its goods or imports and sells them, which means the Japanese brand decides which mark to show; the factory prints it as artwork. Separate obligations under the Containers and Packaging Recycling Act may also apply to your business. Confirm both with your compliance adviser, as of October 2026, and reserve space on the dieline.

Packing and carton marks for Japanese warehouses

Japanese distribution centres commonly specify how cartons must be marked and packed. Ask your warehouse for its rules and pass them to the factory: product code and name, quantity per carton, carton number, lot or order number, JAN barcode where required, maximum carton weight and size, and whether mixed cartons are allowed. Ask for clean inner packing that does not shed fibres onto finished boxes, and a consistent count per carton.

Sample flow and timeline

A typical sequence is digital proof, white (unprinted) structural sample, printed sample, then a pre-production sample checked against the inspection standard in Japan. Because sea and air transit from China to ports such as Tokyo, Yokohama, Nagoya, Osaka and Kobe is relatively short, the schedule is driven mainly by how fast samples are reviewed and approved. Ask your broker about duties, import consumption tax and any preferential treatment available for Chinese-origin goods under trade agreements such as RCEP. For format options, see custom rigid boxes and custom folding cartons.

RFQ checklist for Japanese brands

  1. Structure, inside dimensions and material
  2. Inspection standard and limit samples, or a request to build them together
  3. AQL plan per defect class
  4. Colour references (DIC or Pantone) and finish
  5. Japanese artwork as print-ready PDF, with identification mark positions
  6. Carton marking rules from your warehouse
  7. Quantity (from 500 PCS), destination port, Incoterm and required date

Common mistakes

  • Saying “Japanese quality” without a written, photographed standard.
  • Specifying a DIC colour without confirming how it will be mixed.
  • Approving colour on a proof that is not on the final board and finish.
  • Forgetting the paper or plastic mark until the proof stage.
  • Leaving carton marking to the factory’s default format.

Frequently asked questions

What AQL standard do Japanese buyers use for packaging?

Japanese buyers often refer to JIS Z 9015-1, which is aligned with ISO 2859-1. State the inspection level and AQL for each defect class in the purchase specification.

What is a limit sample in packaging inspection?

A limit sample shows the worst condition still acceptable for a defect, such as a speck or scuff. Physical or photographed limit samples help a factory apply a Japanese buyer’s standard consistently.

Do packaging boxes for Japan need a paper mark?

Many paper containers and packaging discarded by households need the paper identification mark, while corrugated cardboard is outside that obligation. The brand decides; confirm with your compliance adviser.

Can a China factory match DIC colours?

Ask how the colour will be mixed. Some printers mix to DIC directly and others convert to a nearest Pantone or custom formula, so approve the colour on the final substrate.
