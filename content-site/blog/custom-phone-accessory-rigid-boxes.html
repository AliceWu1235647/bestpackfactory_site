Custom Phone Accessory Rigid Boxes: A Complete Buying and Spec Guide

Published August 20, 2026 · Buyer guide · By BestPackFactory

Quick answer

Phone accessories need inserts that hold each item in place: flocked EVA slots for earbuds and cables, foam channels for chargers, and a snug tray for cases. Size slots at product plus 1-2 mm, depth at 60-80% of height, and confirm a 60-80 cm drop test on the packed box. MOQ starts at 500-1,000 pieces for rigid formats; samples take 7-10 days and bulk production 20-30 days.

Matching the insert to the accessory

Earbuds need one cutout per earbud plus a case slot, with the charging case standing upright. Chargers and adapters need a channel that grips the body and a groove for the cable to wrap without kinking. Phone cases can sit in a simple greyboard tray with a tight footprint. In every case, slot width should be product plus 1-2 mm and depth 60-80% of product height so items stay put but lift out easily.

Window displays that survive shipping

A window is common for accessories because the product sells itself on shelf. Use PET or PP film of 0.25-0.5 mm, positioned away from fold lines, and confirm stacking strength on the finished box. For rigid boxes, an open-lid display (product visible with the lid open) needs a strong hinge and a lid that stays back; ask for a hinge test of 50+ open/close cycles on the pre-production sample.

Drop protection and field feedback

E-commerce returns are driven by broken accessory packaging, so protect the product, not just the box: the insert should isolate the heaviest item from the outer wall by at least 3-5 mm of foam or honeycomb. Require a packed drop test at 60-80 cm on all six faces, plus a stacking test with the shipping carton. Ask the factory for the test report, not just a verbal pass.

MOQ, lead time and SKU strategy

Rigid formats start at 500-1,000 pieces per SKU; folding formats with EVA inserts start around 3,000. Samples take 7-10 days; bulk 20-30 days after sign-off. If you launch multiple SKUs, share the shell and swap inserts to cut tooling and keep the per-SKU MOQ realistic for accessories with short lifecycles.

Buyer checklist

  1. Specify slot width (product + 1-2 mm) and depth (60-80% of height) per item
  2. Isolate heavy items from outer walls with 3-5 mm of foam or honeycomb
  3. Request a six-face 60-80 cm drop test with test report
  4. Test window placement and hinge durability (50+ cycles) on samples
  5. Share shells across SKUs and swap inserts to control MOQ

Frequently asked questions

What insert protects true-wireless earbuds?

A flocked EVA insert with a separate cutout for each earbud and the charging case, sized at product plus 1-2 mm and 60-80% depth. This stops rattling and lets the case stand upright in the box.

Do phone accessory boxes need a drop test?

Yes: accessory packaging is a top return driver. Require a six-face drop test at 60-80 cm on a fully packed box and request the test report from the factory before approving bulk.

What MOQ applies to phone accessory boxes?

Rigid formats start at 500-1,000 pieces per SKU; folding cartons with EVA inserts start around 3,000. Sharing one shell across SKUs with different inserts keeps per-SKU volumes realistic.

What the Spec Means for Buyers

Phone accessory rigid boxes need precise cavities and a premium finish. Use 1200/1500/1800 gsm greyboard wrapped in 157 gsm C1S art paper; the accessory cavity is die-cut in EVA foam (20–60 density) from your product dimensions. Corner gap ≤ 1.5 mm, lid-base fit 1.0–1.8 mm. Finishes include matte lamination, soft-touch, hot foil and spot UV. Offset printing runs 1–6 colors with color ΔE ≤ 3.0. The box passes a 24 h static load test and ships at MOQ 500 PCS per size and artwork.
