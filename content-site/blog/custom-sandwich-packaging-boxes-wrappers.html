Custom Sandwich Packaging: Boxes and Wrappers for Freshness and Branding

Published August 22, 2026 · Buyer guide · By BestPackFactory

Quick answer

Select the format by sales channel: wrappers for speed and cost, boxes for stacking and display, or a hybrid with a sleeve. Confirm grease resistance, closure, labeling area and food-contact materials. Test with the real sandwich at expected holding time before mass production.

Match format to operation

A deli wrapping hundreds of sandwiches per day needs a fast wrapper. A café displaying sandwiches needs a window box. Compare material cost, assembly time, storage volume and how the package keeps the sandwich fresh during the holding time.

Grease and moisture are the test

Wrappers and boxes must resist oil from fillings without soaking through. Confirm the coating and paper weight, then test with the greasiest sandwich you sell at the longest holding time.

Labeling and menu flexibility

If ingredients change often, design a label area or use a blank panel instead of printing menu details on the pack. Confirm label adhesion on the coated surface.

Food-contact and reorder control

Request material declarations and food-contact documentation for your market. Keep an approved sample and specification for consistent repeat orders.

Buyer checklist

  1. Match format to service speed
  2. Test grease resistance with real product
  3. Design label area for menu changes
  4. Confirm food-contact materials
  5. Approve a packed sample

Frequently asked questions

Wrapper or box: which is cheaper?

Wrappers usually cost less per unit and store flat, but compare assembly and perceived quality for your channel.

Can sandwich packaging be printed in full color?

Yes, with food-safe inks; confirm the print resists grease and moisture.

How do I keep sandwiches fresh in boxes?

Test venting, coating and holding time; some operations add an inner film or breathable window.

What the Spec Means for Buyers

Sandwich packaging is about food-contact safety and grease performance. Custom food packaging uses kraft or white cardboard at 250–400 gsm with food-safe coatings, printed offset, flexo or digital at 1–8 colors.
