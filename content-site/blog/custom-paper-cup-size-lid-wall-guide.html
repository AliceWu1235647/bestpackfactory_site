How to Choose Paper Cup Size, Rim Diameter, Lid or Sealing Film and Wall Type

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished October 30, 2026Reviewed October 30, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Quick answer

Start from the serving volume on your menu, then pick a cup whose nominal capacity leaves headroom below the brim. The rim diameter of that cup decides which lids or sealing film will fit, so lock the rim size and the closure together. For hot coffee, choose double or ripple wall, or single wall with a sleeve; for iced drinks and sealed milk tea, single wall is usually enough. Confirm capacity, rim fit and seal on a physical sample before printing.

Typical cup sizes and rim diameters

The figures below are common market sizes, not a fixed standard. Rim diameter depends on the cup mould, so two 12 oz cups from different suppliers can take different lids. Always confirm the rim on a sample.

One US fluid ounce is about 29.6 ml and one UK (imperial) fluid ounce is about 28.4 ml, so state the market or simply give the volume in ml.

Step 1: Size from the serving, not from the label

The oz number printed on a cup is usually its nominal serving size. Brim-full capacity is typically higher, because a drink is never filled to the very top. For coffee you also need room for milk foam and for the lid dome; for milk tea you need room for ice, toppings such as tapioca pearls and the sealing film. A practical rule:

  1. Write down the finished drink volume you actually serve, in ml, including ice and toppings.
  2. Add headroom so the liquid line sits a few millimetres below the rim (more for drinks that are carried or delivered).
  3. Choose the nominal size that covers that figure, then ask the supplier for the brim capacity of that cup.
  4. Fill a sample cup with your real recipe and check the fill line before approving.

Many shops use one rim diameter across two or three sizes (for example 12, 16 and 20 oz on the same rim) so that one lid or one film width serves the whole menu. If you can, plan your size range around a shared rim.

Step 2: Match the closure to the rim

Lid fit is a dimensional problem. A lid that is a fraction of a millimetre off will leak or pop off when the cup is squeezed. Rim diameter is measured across the outer rolled edge (the rim curl), because that is where the lid locks on, and 12 oz cups in particular are made on different rims by different suppliers. The same applies to sealing film: the cup rim must suit the sealing machine and film you use.

ClosureBest forWhat to confirm
Sip lid (snap-on)Hot coffee and tea, takeawayRim diameter, rim curl profile, leak test when tilted
Flat or dome lid with straw holeIced coffee, cold drinks, drinks with creamDome height clears toppings, straw hole size
Heat-sealed filmMilk tea and bubble tea, deliverySealing machine model, film width or roll, seal temperature, peel or straw-pierce behaviour

Choose lids when customers drink on the move and want to sip; choose sealing film when drinks travel in delivery bags and must not spill, and when a wide straw is used. If you already own lids or a sealing machine, send a sample lid, the film specification or the machine model with your request, so the cup is matched to what you have rather than the other way round.

Step 3: Single, double or ripple wall

Wall typeHow it is builtChoose it when
Single wallOne paperboard wall with an inner coatingCold or iced drinks, sealed milk tea, or hot drinks served with a sleeve
Double wallA second paper wrap creates an insulating gapHot coffee held without a sleeve; a smooth, premium print surface is wanted
Ripple wallA corrugated outer wrap over the inner cupHot drinks where grip and insulation matter most; a textured look is acceptable

Decision rules that work in most cases:

  • Hot coffee, takeaway: double wall or ripple wall, or single wall plus a sleeve if you want one cup for hot and cold.
  • Iced coffee and milk tea: single wall is usually enough; insulation matters less than condensation and seal quality.
  • Cost-sensitive high volume: single wall with sleeves only for hot orders.
  • Detailed artwork: double wall gives a smooth print surface; ripple texture breaks up fine lines and small text, so proof it on a sample.

For cold drinks, condensation on the outside of the cup can soften the paper over time. Ask how the coating and board handle a cold drink held for the time your customers typically take, and test it on a sample.

Step 4: Choose the inner coating

The inner coating is the barrier that keeps the drink from soaking into the board. PE, PLA and water-based coatings behave differently with heat, oil and local disposal rules. What to send in your RFQ

  1. Drink type and temperature: hot, cold or iced, and how long it is held before drinking.
  2. Serving volume per size in ml, and the oz market you sell in.
  3. Closure: lid type or sealing film; your existing lid sample, film specification or sealing machine model.
  4. Wall type per size, and whether sleeves are needed.
  5. Preferred inner coating or the rule in your market.
  6. Artwork as vector AI or PDF on the supplier's cup-wrap template.
  7. Quantity per size and per design, packing and destination.

Common mistakes

  • Ordering by oz only. Nominal size says nothing about rim diameter or brim capacity.
  • Buying lids and cups from different sources without a fit test. Rim curl profiles differ between moulds.
  • Ignoring the sealing machine. Film that does not suit the machine or rim will leak in delivery.
  • Putting fine text on ripple wall. The corrugated surface distorts small type.
  • Forgetting ice and toppings. A 500 ml recipe with ice does not fit a cup with 500 ml brim capacity.
  • Placing key artwork on the seam. The side seam and the bottom curl are not good areas for logos or text.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 12 oz paper cup really 12 oz to the brim?

Usually not. The oz figure is commonly the nominal serving size, and brim-full capacity is typically higher. Ask the supplier for the brim capacity and test your real recipe in a sample cup.

Do all 12 oz cups take the same lid?

No. Lid fit is set by the rim diameter and rim curl of the cup mould, which vary between suppliers. Confirm the fit on a physical sample before ordering cups and lids together.

Should milk tea cups use lids or sealing film?

Sealing film is common for milk tea and bubble tea, especially for delivery, because it resists spills and works with a wide straw. Lids suit drinks that customers sip on the move.

When is double wall better than single wall with a sleeve?

Double wall suits shops that serve mostly hot drinks and want one comfortable cup without a separate sleeve. Single wall with a sleeve suits menus that mix hot and cold drinks in the same cup.
