Roll Stock Film Spec Sheet: What to Give Your Supplier for VFFS and HFFS Machines

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished October 30, 2026Reviewed October 30, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Quick answer

Give the supplier your machine make and model and a one-page spec covering: film structure and thickness, web width, repeat (cut-off) length, core inner diameter, maximum roll outer diameter or weight, eye mark size, colour and position, unwind direction diagram, seal type (fin or lap) and sealing window, and splice limits. Most of these values come from your packing machine and its forming set, so take them from the machine manual or the machine supplier and confirm them with a test roll.

Why roll stock needs a machine-first spec

Pre-made pouches are checked against a drawing. Roll stock is different: the printed film is formed, filled and sealed on your own machine, so the film has to match that machine’s forming set, sensor, unwind and sealing jaws. A beautiful print that is 5 mm too wide, wound the wrong way or carrying an eye mark the sensor cannot see will stop the line. VFFS (vertical form-fill-seal) machines are typical for snacks, powders and granules; HFFS or flow-wrap machines are typical for bars, biscuits and single items. The spec sheet below works for both. If you are buying labels on rolls instead of film, our roll label winding checklist covers that case.

Web width and repeat length

On a VFFS pillow bag, the film wraps around a forming tube and is sealed lengthwise at the back. A rough calculation is: web width ≈ 2 × bag width + seal allowance. For a fin seal the allowance is two fin widths, often around 10 mm each; for a lap seal it is the overlap, often in the 10–15 mm range. Gusseted and block-bottom bags need extra width for the gussets. The repeat length equals the finished bag length including top and bottom seals. These are approximate; the forming set defines the real figure, so ask your machine supplier for the web width they built the tube for. On HFFS flow-wrap the logic is similar, with the bag wrapping around the product instead of a tube.

Eye mark and registration

  • One eye mark per repeat, printed in a solid, dark colour on a light, clean background, or as your sensor requires.
  • Commonly a rectangle a few millimetres along the web and roughly 10 mm or more across; confirm the size with your sensor range.
  • Keep a clear lane across the web around the mark so no other artwork triggers a false read.
  • Position it where the sensor sits, often near the cut-off or seal, so it ends up in the seal area or a non-display zone.
  • For clear or metallised films, confirm the sensor can read the contrast; a white backing behind the mark often helps.

Unwind direction and print side

Write the direction on a numbered diagram, not in words. State whether the printed face is outside or inside the roll and whether the top or bottom of the design comes off the roll first. Most laminated films are reverse printed on the outer film and buried in the laminate, so the print looks correct from the outside of the bag. Send a photo of the current roll on your machine if you are switching suppliers.

Fin seal or lap seal

A fin seal joins the inside surface to the inside surface and works with most laminates. A lap seal overlaps the edges and joins the inside surface to the outside surface, which saves some film but needs both surfaces to seal together, so the structure has to be designed for it. Tell the supplier which one your machine uses before the structure is chosen.

Choosing the print process

Long runs of roll stock are often printed by rotogravure, while shorter runs and test rolls may be printed digitally. The trade-offs are covered in our guide to digital vs rotogravure printing for flexible packaging.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting the bag width instead of the web width.
  • Sending artwork without the eye mark, or placing text inside the eye mark lane.
  • Ordering rolls larger or heavier than the unwind can hold.
  • Choosing a film for barrier alone and finding it will not seal at the line speed.
  • Skipping a test roll before a full run.

Run a test roll

Before the full order, run a short roll on the real machine at production speed. Record tracking, registration, seal integrity, leakers, the cut position against the print and any static or slip problems. Once it runs well, write the approved width, repeat, core, roll size, eye mark and unwind into the purchase order so every repeat order matches.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate web width for a VFFS pillow bag?

For a fin seal, web width is roughly twice the bag width plus two fin allowances. For a lap seal, it is roughly twice the bag width plus the overlap. Confirm the allowance with your forming tube supplier, because the forming set determines the final value.

What core size is used for roll stock film?

Packing machines commonly take a 76 mm (3 inch) core, and some larger machines use 152 mm (6 inch). Check your machine manual and state the core inner diameter and material in the RFQ.

Where should the eye mark be placed?

The eye mark is usually printed once per repeat in a solid, high-contrast colour, in a clear lane near the cut or seal area where the machine sensor reads it. Your machine supplier should confirm size, position and colour against the sensor.

Can a lap seal be used with any film?

No. A lap seal bonds the inside of the film to the outside, so both surfaces must seal to each other. Fin seals bond inside to inside and work with most laminates.
