Cold Brew Coffee Packaging: Bags, Pouches and Freshness Considerations

Published August 24, 2026 · Buyer guide · By BestPackFactory

Quick answer

Cold brew formats range from RTD cans and bottles to bag-in-box concentrates and single-serve pouches. Confirm the barrier for the format, cold-chain behavior, seal integrity and labeling for storage and dates. Test at your real storage temperature before production.

Format drives the packaging spec

RTD products need a different barrier and filling process than concentrates or ground coffee for home cold brew. Define the format, serving size and distribution temperature before choosing the pack.

Cold behavior and condensation

Chilled packs face condensation and flexing in transit. Confirm seal integrity, label adhesion in cold conditions and carton packing that protects the pack.

Barrier and shelf-life claims

Confirm the barrier structure supports your shelf-life claim at the stated storage temperature. Ask for measured values and validate with a storage test before making claims.

Labeling and compliance

Storage instructions, dates and serving guidance are common requirements. Confirm labeling plans and destination-market rules with compliance advice.

Buyer checklist

  1. Define format and serving size
  2. Confirm barrier for shelf life
  3. Test cold condensation behavior
  4. Verify label adhesion when cold
  5. Approve filled samples

Frequently asked questions

What pack is best for cold brew concentrate?

Pouches with spouts or bag-in-box formats suit concentrates; confirm barrier, spout and seal integrity.

Does cold brew need special film?

The barrier must match your shelf-life claim at storage temperature; confirm measured values.

Why do labels peel on cold packs?

Cold and condensation reduce adhesion; specify cold-tolerant adhesives and test in real conditions.

What the Spec Means for Buyers

Cold brew requires a bag that keeps grounds in contact with water without leaking oxygen in. A factory standard for cold brew is a 250g–1kg side-gusset or flat-bottom bag with an OTR of ≤ 0.8 cc/m²·day and WVTR ≤ 0.8 g/m²·day using a PET 12µm / AL 7µm / PE 80µm or MOPP 20µm / VMPET 12µm / PE 80µm laminate. A one-way degassing valve Ø9–12 mm is positioned 35–55 mm below the top seal so freshly roasted coffee can degas without opening the pack. Seal strength at the side seal and bottom gusset should reach ≥ 35 N / 15 mm, and the filled bag should pass a 1.0 m drop test before export.

Frequently Asked Questions

What barrier values should a cold brew coffee bag meet?

A high-barrier cold brew bag is typically specified at OTR ≤ 0.8 cc/m²·day and WVTR ≤ 0.8 g/m²·day, achievable with a PET / AL / PE or MOPP / VMPET / PE laminate.

Where should the degassing valve be placed?

The one-way valve is normally centered 35–55 mm below the top seal so the bag degases during storage without letting air in.
