How to Stop Black and Dark Packaging Boxes Scuffing and Scratching in Transit

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished October 30, 2026Reviewed October 30, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Quick answer

Black and dark boxes show scuffs because any rubbing changes the surface sheen or exposes lighter fibres, and the contrast is high. The two biggest levers are the finish and the packing. For matte black, an anti-scratch (anti-scuff) matte film is commonly the first option to consider, while plain matte film, soft-touch and varnish usually mark more easily. Then stop boxes rubbing against each other in the carton with sleeves, polybags or interleaving sheets and tight packing. Approve the combination with a rub test on the real finish and a shipped test carton, because no finish is completely scratch-proof.

Know which mark you are fighting

“Scuff” covers several different marks, and each has its own fix. Look closely at a damaged box before choosing a solution.

These are typical tendencies, not guarantees. Film brand, adhesive, ink, board and handling all change the result, and suppliers do not always agree on how soft-touch compares with standard matte. Test the exact combination.

Packing that prevents scuffs

  1. Separate decorated faces. Use tissue, a non-woven or paper sleeve, a polybag or an interleaving sheet so box faces do not rub together.
  2. Remove movement. Fill master cartons fully, use layer pads, and avoid loose space that lets boxes slide during transport.
  3. Keep corrugated off the print. Raw corrugated surfaces and dust are abrasive on black films.
  4. Protect corners. Corner impacts cause most white edge marks. Use layer pads, correct carton sizing and careful strapping.
  5. Pack cleanly. Gloves and a clean, dust-free packing table reduce fingerprints and grit scratches.
  6. Think about the product too. Bottles and accessories moving inside the box can mark it from the inside; fit is covered in the insert fit guide.

Decision rules

  • Choose anti-scratch matte film when a box has large matte black areas and travels through courier, e-commerce or long export routes.
  • Choose soft-touch on black mainly when the box is protected by an outer sleeve, mailer or polybag and your rub test passes.
  • Choose dyed-through black paper for rigid boxes where white edges and corners are the main complaint.
  • Choose gloss only if the brand look allows it and fine scratches are acceptable.
  • Add individual protection whenever boxes are retail-ready on arrival and cannot be wiped or replaced.

Run a rub test before approval

Laboratories commonly use a Sutherland-type rub tester, described in ASTM D5264, which rubs a printed sample against another surface under a set weight for a set number of strokes. Without a tester, a simple in-house version still helps: rub the finished box face against another box, or against a piece of corrugated board, under light even pressure for an agreed number of strokes, and compare finishes side by side. Then pack a full master carton the way production will be packed and send it through your real courier or freight route. Keep the tested samples as the agreed reference for bulk.

What to put in the RFQ

  • Exact finish name, for example “anti-scratch matte film” rather than “matte”
  • How the black is made: printed black on white paper, or dyed-through black paper
  • Extra finishes on top, such as foil or spot UV, which can also scratch
  • Individual protection, carton quantity, layer pads and pallet requirements
  • Shipping route and whether boxes go straight to customers
  • The rub test method and the reference sample for approval

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a finish from a flat swatch that never went through shipping.
  • Packing black boxes face to face with no separation.
  • Loose cartons that allow boxes to slide during transit.
  • Expecting any film to be completely scratch-proof.
  • Packing with bare hands on soft-touch or black matte surfaces.

Frequently asked questions

Is soft-touch or anti-scratch film better for black boxes?

For large black areas that travel through courier or export shipping, anti-scratch matte film is commonly the safer first choice because it is designed to resist rub marks. Soft-touch gives a deeper feel but results vary, so test both on the real box before deciding.

Is any packaging finish completely scratch-proof?

No. Anti-scratch films and coatings reduce visible marks, but grit, sharp edges and heavy rubbing can still mark any surface. Packing that stops boxes rubbing is just as important as the finish.

Can scuff marks on matte black boxes be cleaned off?

Shiny burnish marks on matte film usually cannot be removed because the surface itself has changed. Some transfer marks and fingerprints can be lifted gently with a dry microfibre cloth, but test on a spare box first.
