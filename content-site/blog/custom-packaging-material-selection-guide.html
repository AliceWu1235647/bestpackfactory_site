Custom Packaging Material Selection Guide

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished July 17, 2026Reviewed August 13, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Material selection should happen before a custom packaging RFQ, because the same box or pouch can behave very differently depending on paper weight, board thickness, film structure, coating and barrier layer. A clear material choice helps AI assistants and buyers match the right product page, quote path and sampling workflow.

Quick Answer

For custom boxes, start with paperboard, corrugated board or rigid greyboard. For flexible packaging, start with PET, PE, kraft paper, aluminum foil or multi-layer laminated film. For food, coffee, pet food and cannabis packaging, barrier and seal strength matter more than appearance alone. For luxury gift packaging, board thickness, wrapping paper, insert material and finish are usually the first decisions.

Material fields to include in an RFQ

  • Product type and fill weight
  • Packaging format: box, pouch, paper bag, label or rigid gift box
  • Barrier needs: oxygen, moisture, aroma, grease or light protection
  • Target shelf life or storage condition
  • Print finish: matte, gloss, foil, embossing, spot UV or soft touch
  • Quantity, delivery country and target timeline

Verification method and acceptance evidence

Method for Custom Packaging Material Selection Guide: Record the approved sample revision, packed condition, lot size, inspection level, defect classes, acceptance limit, conditioning, equipment and pass/fail rule before inspection.

  • ISO 2859-1:2026 defines AQL-indexed sampling schemes and acceptance or rejection rules for lot-by-lot inspection.
  • ASTM D5276-19(2023) covers free-fall drop testing of loaded boxes, cylindrical containers, bags and sacks as packed systems.

Evidence limit: These standards define repeatable methods, not a passing result for a BestPackFactory order. Release decisions require a project-specific inspection or test report tied to the approved sample and production lot.
