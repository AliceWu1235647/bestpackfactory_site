Why Hot Foil Cracks or Flakes on Box Fold Lines, and How to Design Around It

Written by Lisa Wu, Sales Manager & Packaging Project AdvisorPublished October 30, 2026Reviewed October 30, 2026

Lisa coordinates B2B packaging specifications, quotations, sampling and production communication with the BestPackFactory team.

Quick answer

Hot foil is a very thin metallic layer with almost no stretch, so when a carton or rigid-box wrap is bent through 90 degrees the outside of the fold stretches and the foil can split, show white fibre or flake off. The most reliable prevention is in the artwork: keep foil off crease and wrap lines, typically by at least 2 to 3 mm, and avoid large solid foil areas that run across a fold. Where foil must cross a fold, specify the board, grain direction, crease size and foil type for that job and approve a folded sample, not a flat sheet.

Foil cracking is one of the few print defects that only appears after the box is formed. The flat sheet looks perfect; the problem shows up on the folding line, the lid edge or the corner of a rigid box once the board is bent. This guide explains why it happens and what to put in the artwork and specification so it does not. For foil types, dies and minimum line widths, see our hot foil stamping guide.

Why foil cracks on a fold

When paperboard is folded, the outer surface is stretched and the inner surface is compressed. Coated board, printed ink, laminate film and hot foil all sit on that outer surface. Foil and many coatings stretch very little, so they fracture before the fibres underneath do, or the fibres themselves split and lift the foil with them. The visible result is a fine line of cracks, white board showing through, or foil lifting in flakes along the fold.

The main contributing factors are well known in carton printing:

  • Foil placed on or near the crease. The closer the foil is to the centre of the fold, the more stretch it sees.
  • Folding against the grain. Paperboard has a grain direction. Folds made against the grain are generally stiffer and more likely to crack on the surface than folds along the grain. Grain direction on a carton is also chosen for stiffness and machine running, so it is a trade-off the factory has to balance.
  • Crease too narrow, too deep or too shallow for the board. The crease rule and the counter (matrix) channel need to match the board caliper. A badly matched crease concentrates stress at the surface instead of creating a clean hinge.
  • Dry or brittle board. Board that has lost moisture, for example in a heated warehouse in winter, is less flexible and cracks more easily on folds.
  • Foil over laminate, varnish or soft-touch. Foil adhesion and flexibility depend on the surface. Foil applied over film lamination, UV coating or soft-touch needs a foil grade suited to that surface; a mismatch can lead to poor adhesion that shows first on folds.
  • Heavy solid foil areas. A thin foil line across a fold may survive; a large solid foil panel wrapping around an edge is much more likely to show cracks.

Artwork rules near folds

  1. Put crease lines in the foil layer view. Build the foil as a separate spot layer on the supplier's dieline and check it against crease and fold lines; our guide to dieline line types explains how cut and crease lines are drawn.
  2. Keep a clear margin from creases. A common starting point is 2 to 3 mm from the crease centre line. Folding cartons, tuck flaps and dust flaps are the usual trouble spots; check the tuck end box dieline for where those creases fall.
  3. Break continuous borders at the fold. If a foil frame runs around the box, interrupt it at each crease, or end it on each panel.
  4. Avoid solid foil across rigid-box edges. On a wrapped rigid box, the wrap paper is pulled tight around the greyboard edge. Foil on that edge sees both a sharp bend and rubbing. Keep foil on the flat lid top or sides.
  5. Allow for registration. Foil, print and die-cutting each have their own tolerance. Do not design foil that relies on stopping exactly at a crease.

What to specify when foil must cross a fold

Some designs need foil to wrap around, for example a continuous band on a sleeve or a full-foil carton. In that case the decision belongs in the specification, not just the artwork:

  • Board grade and caliper, and whether a board with better folding performance is needed; see our board grade comparison.
  • Grain direction relative to the folds that carry foil, confirmed with the factory.
  • Foil type suited to the surface: plain coated board, film laminate, soft-touch or varnish.
  • Folded sample approval: ask for the made-up box, folded several times, and inspect the folds under good light.
  • Storage condition: keep flat sheets and finished cartons away from heaters and direct sun before converting and filling, so the board does not dry out.

Decision rules

  • Choose hot foil when the metallic element sits on flat panels with clear space around creases.
  • Choose a split design (foil interrupted at each fold) when you want a border or band effect on a folding carton.
  • Consider metallic ink or a cold-foil effect when a large metallic area must wrap around folds; they behave differently on a fold, so test them on the same board.
  • Choose a rigid box with foil on the lid top rather than on the edges when the box is a premium gift item; see floral rigid gift boxes with gold foil logo for that layout.

Common mistakes

  • Approving foil from a flat proof or flat press sheet only.
  • Drawing a foil frame exactly to the crease line "so it looks continuous".
  • Putting soft-touch lamination and heavy foil together across a fold without asking about foil compatibility.
  • Ignoring storage: very dry winter warehouses make folds more brittle.

What to send in your RFQ

  • Box style and size, with the dieline showing every crease.
  • The foil layer as a separate spot colour, with foil colour and finish.
  • Board type and any coating, lamination or soft-touch.
  • A note of which foil elements cross folds or edges, and a request for a folded or made-up sample.

Frequently asked questions

Why does hot foil crack on the fold of my box?

Foil and surface coatings stretch very little. When the board folds, the outside of the fold stretches, so foil on or near the crease can split, show white fibre or flake. Grain direction, crease size, dry board and the surface under the foil all affect how much.

How far should foil be from a crease line?

A common starting point is about 2 to 3 mm from the crease centre line, but it depends on board, crease and foil. Confirm on a folded sample.

Can foil go across a fold at all?

Thin lines often can if the board, grain direction, crease and foil type suit it, but solid foil areas across folds and rigid-box edges carry a higher risk. Approve a folded or made-up sample before production.

Does soft-touch lamination make foil cracking worse?

It can if the foil is not suited to that surface. Ask the supplier to confirm a foil grade compatible with soft-touch or laminate and test it on a folded sample.
