Compare Structures

Single Wall, Double Wall and Ripple Wall Paper Cups

This comparison explains the common paper cup structures in the market. Which structures, sizes and coatings can be supplied for your order is confirmed in the quotation and on physical samples.

Single wallOne paperboard wall with an inner barrier coating. The simplest structure for cold drinks, and for hot drinks when a sleeve is used.
Double wallA second paper wrap around the cup creates an insulating gap, so a hot cup is more comfortable to hold without a sleeve. Higher material cost than single wall.
Ripple wallA corrugated outer wrap that adds grip and insulation for hot drinks. The textured surface affects how the artwork prints and should be proofed on a sample.
Inner coatingPE, PLA and water-based barrier coatings behave differently in heat, oil and recycling streams. The coating is chosen from your drink, temperature and market rules; see PE vs PLA vs water-based coating.
ClosureFitted lids (for example sip lids) for hot coffee and tea; sealing film with a straw is common for milk tea. Fit is set by the rim diameter.

FAQ

Custom Paper Cup Questions

Can coffee and milk tea use the same paper cup?

Sometimes, but the closure usually decides it. Hot coffee is normally served with a fitted lid, while milk tea is often sealed with film and drunk through a wide straw. Tell us both drinks, the serving temperature and the closure you use, and we confirm whether one cup body can serve both or whether two specifications are needed.

Should I choose single wall or double wall cups?

Single wall is the simplest structure and is commonly paired with a sleeve for hot drinks. Double wall and ripple wall add an outer layer that makes a hot cup more comfortable to hold without a sleeve, at a higher material cost. The structures available for your order are confirmed in the quotation.

How do I make sure the lid or sealing film fits?

Lid and film fit depends on the rim diameter of the cup, not on the nominal capacity. Send the rim diameter or a sample of the lid or sealing machine you already use, and the fit is checked on physical samples before mass production.

How should I check capacity in oz and ml?

Nominal capacity is usually the brim-full volume, while the drink you serve sits lower in the cup. State the serving volume in ml, the brim capacity you expect and the market (US fl oz and UK fl oz differ), so the quotation names the same cup you intend to buy.

How is condensation on cold drinks tested?

Condensation and softening depend on the drink temperature, ice, room humidity and holding time. We agree the test conditions with you and check them on samples; we do not promise a fixed holding time without that test.
